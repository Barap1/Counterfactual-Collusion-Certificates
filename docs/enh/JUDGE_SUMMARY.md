# Same evidence, different verdicts

**The problem.** Removing a message can show that it changed an outcome. Even an outsider's loss does not, by itself, establish collusion: legitimate cooperation can produce the same transcript and replay effects. CCC asks what claim the available evidence licenses, separating suspicious text, causal influence, harmful exposure and justified attribution.

**Why it matters.** Audits can inform sanctions, shutdowns and deployment decisions. Treating a causal effect as blame risks accusing legitimate cooperation. An evidence standard should identify both the supported claim and the additional evidence needed to resolve ambiguity.

**Most impressive demonstrable result — new-prospective [NP].** Executable twin worlds have byte-identical auditor observables but different scripted attribution labels. For colluding allocation versus efficiency-motivated cooperation (P2), the separating frontier is the environment probe (I4); for unauthorized versus authorized transfer (P3), it is the external authorization record (I5). The measured frontier agrees with enumeration. This is an inspectable counterexample within the declared generator and information sets, minimal among the enumerated alternatives. It is a consistency result by construction, not independent detection performance or a general impossibility theorem.

**What was learned.** The evidence contract survives; the proposed auditor's prespecified utility remains unestablished. RQ1 and RQ2 pass by construction; RQ3, RQ4, RQ4b and RQ5 failed; RQ6 is descriptive [NP]. RQ3's point threshold holds only in the noisy stratum, without margin. Neither the original over-attribution prediction nor its DEV-informed revision holds across both required strata. Zero overclaims does not establish useful discrimination when a reporter can abstain. CCC-L remains exploratory-method. End-to-end positive-control certificate recovery and sensitivity are unestablished.

**EXP-002, verbatim from the paper:**

> **Primary [OC].** Delta = 1.354 reward units, world-cluster 95% CI [-0.020, 2.834], exact one-sided sign-flip p = 0.0703 (18/256), n = 8 matched world pairs. Both required criteria fail; the preregistered directional hypothesis is **NOT supported**. Floors were met (8 attempted pairs, 7 nonzero paired effects, 8 eligible complete pairs). This is a failed confirmatory directional test, not evidence that the effect is absent.

Here [OC] means original-confirmatory. EXP-002 compares prompt regimes, not realized collusion, and does not validate the scripted frontier in real agents. EXP-003 was not completed; no result is claimed. The final review also flags the failed cross-platform identity gate and its replacement acceptance rule (D-046); reproducible reporting does not establish cross-platform equivalence.
